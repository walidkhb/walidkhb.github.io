The Problems I Solve

Six problems that hold technology organisations back.

Each one is a leadership problem before it is a technology problem. Each answer below comes from work inside a regulated bank.

01

Fragmented technology

Every product integrated from scratch, every connection a one-off. I replace that with one governed platform the whole bank builds on, so every new product starts from capability that already exists.

Evidence: Integration-as-a-Platform Strategy →
02

Slow delivery, costly change

Manual, person-dependent releases make every change expensive. I made delivery automated, audited and reversible, so change becomes routine instead of risky.

Evidence: Automated Integration Delivery →
03

Vendor dependency

When standards, pipelines and know-how sit with suppliers, the bank rents its own capability. I took managed services from evaluation to implementation and made delivery through the bank’s own pipeline a sourcing requirement.

Evidence: Integration Framework & sourcing →
04

Weak governance and ownership

Unclear ownership and inconsistent standards slow everything down. I authored the bank’s Integration Framework: one set of standards and review gates that every project and vendor works to.

Evidence: Enterprise Integration Framework →
05

Resilience and regulatory pressure

Regulators now expect live connectivity and services that survive a site failure. Instant-payment checks run active-active across two sites, and regulatory and partner programmes share one platform.

Evidence: Regulatory Connectivity Programme →
06

Adopting AI safely

AI creates value only on governed foundations. I built those foundations first, contributed to the bank’s secure-by-design review for AI enablement and called for a bank-wide policy on AI-assisted engineering.

Evidence: AI-Native Readiness →

How I Lead

How I make technology decisions.

Platforms over isolated projects.

Every initiative should leave a reusable capability behind. Projects end; platforms compound.

Clear ownership.

Every capability needs a named owner, an accountable team and a roadmap. Ambiguity is the most expensive architecture.

Governance that speeds delivery.

Standards should make the right thing the easy thing. If a control slows delivery without reducing risk, it is the wrong control.

Architectural discipline.

Decisions are written down, reviewed at the right gate and revisited with evidence—not re-argued in every meeting.

Build capability inside the institution.

Knowledge that lives only with a vendor or a few individuals is a risk. I build the platforms, standards and teams that keep it in the bank.

Strategic sourcing.

Buy what is commodity, build what differentiates, and set the terms suppliers deliver to—never the other way round.

Develop people and leaders.

Organisations scale through leaders who can run without me. I grow them deliberately, with real ownership.

Measure what matters.

Throughput, reliability, cost and regulatory outcomes—reported plainly, including when they are behind plan.
