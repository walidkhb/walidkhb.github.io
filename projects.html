01Platform strategy

Integration-as-a-Platform Strategy

Repositioning integration from a queue of one-off requests into a platform the bank owns.

Period
2019–present
Accountability
Personally led
Role
Integration Manager in 2019; VP, Enterprise Integration since June 2025
  • 40+people in the organisation I lead
  • 129integration services to production in 2026 (89 new)
  • 392production releases in 2026, with go-lives in every month
  • 49new API products in 2026

2026 figures cover 1 January to 11 November.

All case studies ↑
Context

Integration had grown one project at a time. Each new product, partner or regulatory change added another point-to-point connection, built its own way and owned by no one, so change was slow and every release carried hidden risk.

Mandate

Set and then own the bank’s integration strategy: as Integration Manager from 2019, and as head of the function since 2023. Since June 2025, as VP, Enterprise Integration, I lead an organisation of 40+.

Strategic decision

Run integration as a platform the bank owns, with a roadmap, reusable services and governance, instead of a queue of one-off requests. Every new connection should strengthen the platform rather than add another bespoke link.

Execution

I aligned organisation, architecture, delivery and sourcing around that one idea: teams shaped around platform ownership, governed and reusable services across the bank’s core, channel, payment, partner and regulatory systems, and a single delivery pipeline.

On sourcing, I took the managed-services model for integration from evaluation to implementation (scope of work, service levels, resource selection and knowledge transfer). We retired legacy integration platforms, right-sized platform licences and improved cost and performance with a third-party service provider.

Outcome

Time-to-market for new products and partners shortened, and partner onboarding became faster and repeatable. In 2026 the function put 129 integration services into production (89 new), with 392 production releases, go-lives in every month and 49 new API products. Four flagship programmes went live: corporate internet banking, the IVR revamp, a digital partner app and national enforcement services.

Institutional capability

Integration is an owned platform with its own roadmap, standards and governance, not a service-request queue. Identity, investment services, ERP, loyalty, trade finance, corporate KYC and counter-fraud data feeds are all delivered through its services.

Lessons
  • Reuse is decided on the day a project is late. A platform strategy holds only if someone with authority turns down the quick one-off connection.
  • Report the platform by what it ships each month. Steady, visible delivery earns more trust than any target architecture.

02Cloud-native architecture

Cloud-Native Banking Platform

Setting the direction for how new digital services are built, released and kept running.

Period
2021–2026
Accountability
Strategically sponsored
Role
Digital Services Manager in 2021; VP, Enterprise Integration since June 2025
  • 2 sitesrun instant-payment checks active-active
  • 2 data centresrun national enforcement services
All case studies ↑
Context

New digital services could not move at the pace the business needed on monoliths and hand-managed servers, where one release carried everything and a single failure could take a whole service down.

Mandate

Set the architectural direction for new digital builds: first from the Digital Factory, then across integration.

Strategic decision

Containerised microservices on OpenShift with a service mesh, so that services deploy, scale and fail independently and service-to-service traffic is secured by default. Start with one real product, not a platform programme.

Execution

The pattern began with the Digital Factory’s first microservices application and proved itself in production before it was asked to carry more. Resilience was then designed service by service, rather than declared for the estate as a whole.

Outcome

Instant-payment checks run active-active across two sites. National enforcement services run across two data centres. Card one-time passwords no longer depend on core banking, and notifications are served from both sites.

Institutional capability

New digital services start on a proven pattern where deployment, scaling and service security are already solved, and they release on their own schedule instead of all together.

Lessons
  • Earn the standard with one product in production. A working first application persuades more people than a reference architecture.
  • Claim resilience service by service. Being precise about what is protected is what lets risk and operations trust the claim.

03Delivery automation

Automated Integration Delivery Platform

Making release a property of the platform, not of the few people who know the steps.

Period
2024–2026
Accountability
Team-led delivery
Role
Acting Head of Integration; VP, Enterprise Integration since June 2025
  • 12integration platforms on automated build and release
  • 142repositories on one engineering platform
  • ≈2 minfrom merge to a running build
  • 278API products brought under Git control
All case studies ↑
Context

Integration ran on a legacy service bus, deployed by hand one environment at a time. Releases depended on the few people who knew the steps, and audit had to reconstruct what had changed after the fact.

Mandate

Modernise how integration change reaches production, for the bank’s own teams and for the vendors who build on its platforms.

Strategic decision

Make Git the single source of truth: every release versioned, each environment’s configuration in its own repository, API publishing automated. Automate everything up to production, and keep production approval-gated.

Execution

We moved integration onto a container platform and built one engineering platform for delivery, with compliance checks on every change. GitOps is live on development; promotion to higher environments runs by change request. I made delivery through the bank’s own source control, CI/CD and deployment pipeline a requirement in integration modernisation sourcing.

Outcome

Automated build and release for 12 integration platforms, on an engineering platform of 142 repositories and 10 published standards. About two minutes from merge to a running build, and 278 API products brought under Git control. Production deployment stays approval-gated.

Institutional capability

Releases are repeatable and traceable end to end. Operations, audit and vendors read the same record of what changed, when and why.

Lessons
  • Automate the path and keep the decision. In a regulated bank, an approval gate before production is a control to design well, not a gap to apologise for.
  • Write the pipeline into sourcing. If the contract is silent, every vendor brings its own tooling and you inherit all of it.

04Architecture governance

Enterprise Integration Framework & Standards

Setting the rules once, so every team and vendor inherits them.

Period
2024–2026
Accountability
Personally designed
Role
Acting Head of Integration; VP, Enterprise Integration since June 2025
  • 10standards
  • 4review gates, with threat modelling at design
All case studies ↑
Context

Every project and vendor designed integration its own way. Quality depended on who delivered, and the reasons behind architecture decisions lived in email threads and people’s memories.

Mandate

Define how integration is designed, secured, built, tested and governed at the bank, for internal teams and vendors alike.

Strategic decision

A small set of enforceable standards, each tied to a review gate in the delivery lifecycle, with the same gates for vendors as for internal teams. Architecture decisions are recorded, so the reasoning outlives the people who made it.

Execution

I authored the bank’s Integration Framework: ten standards, four review gates with threat modelling at design, and recorded architecture decisions. It is referenced in technology RFPs.

Outcome

The same gates apply to internal teams and vendors. The discipline now reaches wider decisions: I led an architecture and security review of the corporate internet banking estate, with a prioritised remediation plan for executive management. I also contributed the integration and architecture position to strategic reviews of core banking modernisation, the treasury platform, corporate internet banking, the payment hub and Open Banking.

Institutional capability

Integration quality no longer depends on which team or vendor delivers. The bank sets the rules once, and RFPs and reviews refer to them instead of reinventing them.

Lessons
  • A standard without a gate is a suggestion. Tie every rule to a moment in the lifecycle where someone has to sign it off.
  • Put threat modelling at design, where changing course is cheap, not at release, where it becomes a negotiation.

05AI readiness

AI-Native Readiness & Engineering Governance

Building the governed foundations first, so AI can be adopted safely when the bank decides to.

Period
2025–2026
Accountability
Contributed / proposedPersonal practice
Role
VP, Enterprise Integration
Status
Bank-wide policy on AI-assisted engineering proposed
All case studies ↑
Context

AI-assisted engineering is arriving faster than the controls a regulated bank needs around it. Adopted onto ungoverned delivery, it would multiply risk rather than speed.

Mandate

Influence rather than ownership: keep integration engineering ready for AI, and bring the integration view to the bank’s decisions on AI enablement.

Strategic decision

Governance and secured interfaces before tools. Controls that apply to every change, whether a person wrote it or AI assisted, turn adoption into a policy decision instead of a re-platforming.

Execution

The integration engineering platform enforces compliance gates on every change, and its APIs are secured. I contributed the integration view to the bank’s secure-by-design review for AI enablement and called for a bank-wide policy on AI-assisted engineering. Separately, I practise Claude-native engineering myself, to learn first-hand what AI-assisted delivery asks of governance.

Outcome

The governed foundations AI needs are in place on the integration platform, and the policy has been proposed. This is readiness work, not an AI deployment: adoption is the bank’s decision.

Institutional capability

Delivery rails that are already governed, auditable and secure, so AI-assisted delivery would not require rebuilding the foundations.

Lessons
  • In a regulated bank, AI readiness is a governance problem before it is a tooling problem.
  • Use the tools yourself before writing policy for others. First-hand practice makes the policy specific.

06Regulatory connectivity

Regulatory Connectivity Programme

Turning each new mandate into an instance of a shared capability, not a new project.

Period
2023–present
Accountability
Team-led delivery
Role
Acting Head of Integration; VP, Enterprise Integration since June 2025
  • 12+regulatory and partner programmes
  • 12 monthsof SIMAH credit-bureau reporting loaded error-free
  • WeeklySAMA reporting, automated
All case studies ↑
Context

Supervision is moving from periodic reports towards continuous, round-the-clock instructions. Each new mandate used to arrive as its own project, with its own connection, its own risk and its own deadline.

Mandate

Deliver the integration behind the bank’s regulatory and partner obligations: reliably, securely and on the regulator’s timetable.

Strategic decision

Build regulatory connectivity once, as a shared capability, and deliver each mandate on it rather than beside it.

Execution

My teams delivered integration for SAMA’s national enforcement programme (Tanfeeth) across successive waves, credit-bureau reporting to SIMAH and automated weekly SAMA reporting, as part of more than twelve regulatory and partner programmes on the shared platform.

Outcome

Integration for 12+ regulatory and partner programmes. National enforcement services went live in 2026 and run across two data centres. SIMAH reporting is live with twelve months loaded error-free, and weekly SAMA reporting is automated.

Institutional capability

A new mandate lands on a platform that already exists, with known patterns for onboarding, security, monitoring and evidence.

Lessons
  • Treat every mandate as an instance of a capability. If the second one costs as much as the first, you have built projects, not a platform.
  • Regulators judge reliability over time. Design for the twelfth month of reporting, not for go-live.

07Open Banking

Open Banking, Built In-House

Period
2024–2026
Accountability
Personally led
Status
Account information live and certified; payment initiation certification scheduled for December 2026–January 2027
  • 130/130SAMA functional conformance tests passed in sandbox
Context

SAMA’s Open Banking framework needed a platform the bank could own, change and take through certification on its own terms.

Mandate

Decide how the bank would meet the mandate, then lead delivery.

Strategic decision

I led the build-not-buy decision: an in-house authorisation server with account-information and payment-initiation services.

Execution

Built to the FAPI 2.0 security profile and taken through SAMA’s functional conformance suites in sandbox.

Outcome

Passes all 130 SAMA functional conformance tests in sandbox (account information 67/67; payment initiation 32/32 and 31/31). The account information service is live and certified, with the first third-party provider live. Payment initiation certification is scheduled for December 2026–January 2027.

Institutional capability

The bank holds the platform, the security model and the expertise to meet each next phase of the mandate itself.

Lessons
  • Build where the regulation is the product. For the most regulated interface a bank has, owning the security model and the release cadence outweighs the convenience of a package.

08Payments

Payment Hub Integration

Period
2024–2026
Accountability
Team-led delivery
  • 5payment types on one interface into the core
Context

A new payment hub needed one dependable path into core banking for every payment type.

Mandate

Lead the integration layer between the new payment hub and core banking.

Strategic decision

One retry-safe interface into the core for instant payments, SARIE, SADAD, bulk payments and request-to-pay, rather than one per payment type.

Execution

A two-phase design: funds are verified and held before a payment leaves, then posted or released when it completes, so a retry cannot pay twice and every hold has a defined end.

Outcome

One controlled, retry-safe path into core banking for all five payment types, with funds held before a payment goes out and released when it completes.

Institutional capability

Once live, adding a payment type becomes work on a known pattern rather than a new project against the core.

Lessons
  • Design for the retry, not the happy path. In payments, idempotency and a defined end for every hold matter more than raw throughput.

09Core banking access

Core Banking APIs

Period
2024–2026
Accountability
Personally led
  • 14governed business APIs
Context

Every project reached into core banking its own way, and every test cycle queued behind the core.

Mandate

Own how projects consume core banking through integration.

Strategic decision

Treat the core as a product with a governed front door, instead of open direct access.

Execution

Replaced direct access with a catalogue of 14 governed business APIs, each with an owner, a contract and documentation, and a simulator of the core’s behaviour.

Outcome

Projects test in parallel against the simulator instead of queueing for the core.

Institutional capability

Knowledge of how the core behaves lives in contracts and a simulator, not with a few individuals.

Lessons
  • Expose business capabilities, not internal message formats. A small set of governed contracts is easier to change than many direct connections.

12Risk

Real-Time Fraud Monitoring

Period
2023–2025
Accountability
Team-led delivery
Context

Fraud detection ran in overnight batches, so suspicious activity was reviewed after the fact.

Mandate

Change the integration behind fraud detection so monitoring sees transactions as they happen.

Strategic decision

Treat transactions as events evaluated in flight, not records reviewed overnight.

Execution

Transaction events stream to monitoring, with stateful processing over short time windows.

Outcome

Fraud detection moved from overnight batch to real-time transaction monitoring.

Institutional capability

Fraud monitoring works from live transaction events rather than an overnight batch.

Lessons
  • A control is only as useful as its timing. Moving the same checks from batch to stream changes what they can prevent.

14Modernisation

Legacy Modernisation

Period
2018–2019
Accountability
Personally led
Role
Senior Integration Developer
  • Zerodowntime during the migration
Context

The legacy integration platform carried live transactions but was costly to run and could not support modern patterns.

Mandate

Migrate the legacy integration platform while the bank kept running on it.

Strategic decision

Incremental replacement behind a stable interface, with parallel running and reconciliation, instead of a single cut-over.

Execution

Services moved in stages, each checked against the old platform before traffic switched.

Outcome

Lessons
  • Make the migration boring. Small, reversible steps earn a confidence that a big-bang plan never gets.
